### Learned

  comparing to the statutory minimum.** Missing this would have made cost
  oracle O7 fail later (re-applied commission ≠ accumulated commission for

  discovering it mid-oracle-suite kept the fix isolated and easy to review.
- **The dated settlement ledger cannot honour dividend pay-date timing
  without `pay_date` in the schema.** This was identified back in Phase 1
  as an unused-column gap (memory note) and only became load-bearing once
  Phase 5's settlement model needed to distinguish ex-date share mutation
  from pay-date cash arrival.

## 2026-07-03 (Friday)

### Did

- **Step 5** — `FillModel` ABC: `VolumeParticipationFiller` (caps a fill at
  `floor(participation_rate × trailing_adv)`, locked default 5%) and
  `FullFill` (always the full remaining order — the frictionless
  reconciliation-reference preset); `fillable()` takes `remaining` and
  `trailing_adv` as plain values rather than `Order`/`Bar`, so the model
  is structurally unable to reach for a bar's own volume, which would be
  lookahead at the T+1 open

### Learned

- **Keeping the fill model's signature to plain values, not `Order`/`Bar`
  objects, is itself a lookahead guard.** If `fillable()` had received the
  full `Bar`, nothing would stop a future edit from reading
  `bar.volume` (today's full-day volume) instead of the pre-computed
  `trailing_adv` — the narrow signature makes the mistake structurally
  unavailable rather than relying on code review to catch it.

  (Beneficial Certificate Rules Art. 7), not a data quality issue** —

  to miss.

## Retrospective

### What went well

- **Pre-phase steps 0a–0c cleared exactly the gaps they were meant to.**
  Each was traceable to a specific later failure mode (O7 cost
  reconstruction, dividend pay-date settlement, `Account`'s incremental
  lot tracking) and landed in one day without follow-up rework.
- **The event-sourced spine (steps 1–4) came together with no redesign.**
  `EventLog`, `BrokerEvent`, and `fold()` were built in the order the spec
  called for — foundations before mechanics — and every later step so far
  has built on them without needing to revisit their shape.

### What to carry forward

- `EventLog`'s cross-instance append behavior and `fold()`'s
  partially-hydrated-account support were both designed in from step 1 and
  step 4 respectively, well before any concrete need for them existed.
  Worth remembering when later evaluating how much new work "resume"
  support would actually require — the primitives may already be there.
